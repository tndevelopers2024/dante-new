How Much Do Braces Cost in 2026? Full Price Breakdown and Payment Options

Dante Gonzales Orthodontics August 19, 2026 8 min read

On this page

Whether you're researching orthodontic treatment for a child, considering adult treatment for yourself, or simply trying to plan your family's budget for the year ahead, one question comes up first: how much do braces cost? The honest answer is that there is no single number, since the cost of braces depends on several factors — the type of braces you choose, the complexity of your case, your location, your orthodontist's experience, and your insurance coverage. At Dante Gonzales Orthodontics, we believe an informed decision starts with real numbers, so here is a full breakdown of braces cost in 2026, what drives the price up or down, and the payment options available to make treatment affordable.

What Is the Average Cost of Braces?

The average cost of orthodontic treatment in the United States ranges from $3,000 to $8,000, though the total cost for your specific case will vary based on multiple factors, including the type of braces, treatment duration, and where you live.

As a regional example, braces in Chicago typically cost between $3,000 and $7,500 — a helpful illustration of how geographic location affects orthodontic fees due to differences in local cost of living, office overhead, and demand.

What Affects the Cost of Braces?

Several factors play into the final price you'll pay at the orthodontist's office. Understanding these variables helps explain why one patient's treatment costs significantly more than another's.

Type of braces. Traditional metal braces remain the most affordable and durable option, while ceramic braces cost slightly more due to their tooth-colored brackets. Lingual braces, which use clear brackets placed behind the teeth, carry the highest fees because of the specialized technique required to place and adjust them.

Case complexity. Complex cases involving significant misalignment, bite correction, or crowded and misaligned teeth require more adjustments, more office visits, and a longer treatment time — all of which increase the total cost. Minor corrections, by contrast, typically fall on the lower end of the price range.

Treatment duration. Treatment duration typically lasts 12 to 36 months, and longer treatment plans generally cost more due to the additional appointments and materials involved over time.

Geographic location. Braces in urban areas are generally more expensive than in smaller towns, largely due to higher overhead costs for the orthodontist's office.

Orthodontist's experience. An orthodontist's experience and reputation can influence treatment costs, with highly experienced providers sometimes charging higher fees for their expertise.

Age of the patient. Adult braces can cost $500 to $1,500 more than children's braces. This premium exists partly because adult patients may experience slower tooth movement, requiring more careful monitoring and sometimes a longer treatment time to move teeth into proper position safely.

Does Insurance Cover Braces?

Many patients ask whether dental insurance will help cover the cost of braces, and the answer is: it depends on your plan. Most insurance plans cover $1,000 to $3,000 of braces costs, though dental insurance may cover part of orthodontic treatment with lifetime caps that fall within that same $1,000 to $3,000 range. Once you hit that lifetime maximum, any additional orthodontic costs — including a second round of treatment later in life — are typically paid out of pocket.

Here's what to keep in mind about insurance coverage for braces:

  • Most PPO plans cover approximately 50% of braces costs, up to the plan's lifetime maximum
  • Insurance coverage for orthodontic treatment can differ substantially between children and adults, with insurance coverage for adult braces often more limited than coverage for children
  • Waiting periods for orthodontic coverage are common, often lasting 6 to 12 months before benefits become available
  • Orthodontic benefits are sometimes only available if treatment is deemed medically necessary rather than purely cosmetic
  • Checking whether your orthodontist is in-network with your insurance provider can significantly affect your out-of-pocket total cost

Because insurance coverage varies significantly by plan, it's worth calling your insurance provider directly, or asking your orthodontist's office to verify your specific orthodontic benefits before starting treatment. Most patients still pay between $5,000 and $7,000 out of pocket before insurance is applied, so understanding your plan's lifetime maximum ahead of time helps you plan your budget realistically.

Payment Options and Financing for Braces

Braces are a significant investment, but most orthodontic clinics are structured to make treatment accessible regardless of your budget. Common payment options include:

  • Monthly payment plans, often with 0% interest, that spread the total cost over the length of treatment
  • A down payment of $500 to $1,000, with the remaining balance divided into manageable monthly payments
  • Health Savings Accounts (HSA), which let you set aside pre-tax dollars for orthodontic costs
  • Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA), which can reduce your effective braces cost by using pre-tax dollars for treatment
  • Free initial consultations, offered by many orthodontists, which provide a personalized cost estimate before you commit to anything
  • In-house financing directly through the orthodontist's office, which can sometimes offer more flexible terms than third-party lenders

Comparing quotes from multiple providers is one of the most effective ways to save money, since fees can vary meaningfully between practices even within the same city.

Are There Additional Costs Beyond the Braces Themselves?

When budgeting for orthodontic care, it helps to plan for a few additional costs beyond the base price of braces:

  • Initial records, X-rays, and diagnostic imaging
  • Retainers following the completion of active treatment
  • Emergency visits for a broken bracket or loose wire
  • Routine adjustment appointments throughout treatment
  • Ongoing at-home orthodontic care supplies

Most orthodontists build these into the total cost quoted upfront, but it's always worth asking your orthodontist's office directly so there are no surprises once you begin.

Is the Cost of Braces Worth It?

Beyond a confident smile, orthodontic treatment supports long-term oral health. Misaligned teeth and crowded or misaligned teeth are harder to clean effectively, which raises the risk of tooth decay and gum disease over time. Correcting these orthodontic issues doesn't just improve appearance — it also protects your dental health for years to come, which is part of why many patients view the cost of braces as a worthwhile, long-term investment rather than a purely cosmetic expense.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. Can braces be fully covered by insurance?

Full coverage is uncommon. Most insurance plans cover $1,000 to $3,000 of braces costs as a lifetime maximum, meaning patients typically still pay a significant portion of the total cost out of pocket.

2. What age are braces free?

Braces are not free at any age through a standard orthodontist's office, though some public health programs or state Medicaid plans may cover orthodontic treatment for qualifying children when deemed medically necessary.

3. Is insurance worth it for braces?

For many patients, yes. Even a partial benefit, such as 50% coverage up to a $1,000–$3,000 lifetime maximum, can meaningfully reduce out-of-pocket costs, especially when combined with an FSA or HSA to cover the remaining balance with pre-tax dollars.

4. How to get braces if you can't afford it?

Options include monthly payment plans with 0% interest offered by many orthodontic clinics, using FSA or HSA funds, comparing quotes from multiple providers, asking about in-house financing, and checking whether your dental insurance plan includes any orthodontic benefits.

5. Is $5,000 a good price for braces?

Yes, $5,000 falls within the typical range for traditional metal braces and is close to the amount most patients pay out of pocket before insurance, especially for cases of average complexity.

6. Can you pay $100 a month for braces?

It's possible, depending on the total treatment cost, down payment, and length of the payment plan. Many orthodontic offices structure monthly payments to fit different budgets, so ask your orthodontist's office about specific monthly payment options during your consultation.

7. How are people affording braces?

Most patients combine a few strategies: partial insurance coverage, a manageable down payment, 0% interest monthly payment plans, and pre-tax FSA or HSA dollars to reduce the effective cost.

8. Can braces cost $10,000?

Yes, particularly for lingual braces, which can cost $8,000 to $10,000 or more, or for complex cases requiring a longer treatment time and more extensive correction.

9. How much do 2 years of braces cost?

Since treatment duration is already factored into the total quoted cost rather than billed separately by year, two years of braces would typically fall within the standard $3,000 to $8,500 range depending on the type of braces and case complexity, rather than being priced as an additional yearly fee.

10. What is the cheapest price to get braces?

Traditional metal braces are generally the most budget-friendly option, with costs starting around $3,000 for simpler cases with minor corrections.

11. Is it worth getting braces at 30?

Yes. Adult treatment is common and effective at any age. While adult braces can cost slightly more than children's braces due to slower tooth movement, treatment can still successfully correct misaligned teeth and support long-term oral health regardless of age.

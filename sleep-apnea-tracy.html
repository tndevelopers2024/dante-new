What sleep apnea actually does to your night

Most people don’t realise how common sleep apnea is until the tiredness becomes constant, or a partner mentions the snoring. It’s a quiet problem that causes more than poor sleep — left untreated it is linked to high blood pressure, stroke and heart attack.

When a person with sleep apnea sleeps, the throat muscles relax too much. The soft tissue in the throat collapses, blocking the airway for a few seconds at a time. The brain then forces the body to wake and start breathing again. This cycle may repeat dozens to a hundred of times per night. This leads to very little real sleep.

Common Causes and How We Determine Treatment

During your first visit, we review your symptoms and take X-rays or airway scans to determine how your jaw and mouth behave while you sleep. Sometimes jaw forward movement improves the airway naturally. For others, excess soft tissue or weak throat muscles need targeted correction. Every case is different, and we make sure to tailor the care. That’s how we see it anyway.

Our treatment options vary based on your health and comfort. For some, a custom oral appliance keeps the jaw forward slightly, opening the airway and allowing smooth breathing all night. Others combine orthodontic adjustments with weight loss or CPAP therapy for full recovery. It’s about what works for your body.

How Sleep Apnea Affects Overall Health

Untreated apnea affects every part of the human body. Low oxygen levels make the brain work harder. Over time, it can cause fatigue, irritability, and poor focus. The lack of deep sleep means the body never rests completely. Even throat dryness and headaches appear frequently.

Patients often describe waking suddenly, feeling like they are choking or gasping. That’s the brain trying to restart breathing. Over months, this stress increases the risk of heart attack or stroke. It also affects metabolism, making weight loss harder. It’s not just snoring, it’s your body signalling distress.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What causes sleep apnea?

It happens when throat muscles and soft tissue block the airway for a few seconds during sleep. This interrupts breathing and lowers oxygen levels.

2. How does Dr Dante Gonzales treat sleep apnea?

We use oral appliances that keep the jaw forward to open the airway and improve nighttime breathing. Other treatment options are reviewed if needed.

3. Can untreated sleep apnea cause other health issues?

Yes. It may lead to high blood pressure, heart attack, stroke, and daytime irritability. Proper treatment can restore healthy sleep and energy.

4. Is sleep apnea treatment painful or uncomfortable?

Not at all. Most patients find our treatments gentle and easy to adapt to. The oral appliance fits comfortably and doesn’t interfere with regular sleep. Within a few nights, it often feels completely natural.

5. How soon can I expect results from treatment?

Many patients notice better sleep and more energy within the first few weeks. The exact timeline depends on your condition, but consistent use of the appliance or other recommended therapies leads to steady, lasting improvement.
